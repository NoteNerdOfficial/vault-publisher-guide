Pages the build writes for you

Updated 8 Nov 2026Links 7 in · 4 out#feature#navigation#site

A note becomes a page. But most of what is on the screen was written by the build rather than by you, and none of it needs configuring.

The front page

If one of your notes should be the homepage, say so under Homepage and that note is published at index.html rather than at its own URL, so every link to it, from wikilinks to backlinks to the tree to the graph, points at the front page rather than at a second copy of it.

Leave it blank and the build looks for README.md, then index.md, then Home.md, among the notes being published. Failing all three it writes an overview of its own, carrying:

  • the note count and the tag count
  • Start here, from your pinned notes, in the order you numbered them
  • Most linked, ordered by how much of the vault points at each note
  • Recently updated, by the date property
  • Tags, as a cloud
  • Browse by folder
This vault

Start here.md is the homepage, set explicitly. Build the preview and it is what opens, at index.html, with no second copy of it anywhere.

A page per tag

Every tag in the published set gets its own page listing the notes that carry it, plus a tag index. The chips in a note's header link to them.

The browse page

The vault's folder structure, as a tree, for a reader who would rather look around than search.

On every note page

Across the top. A command bar with search. Search or jump to… covers every published note, and the drop-down offers Recently updated before you have typed anything.

Down the left. Start here above the vault tree. Your pinned notes sit at the top of the panel and stay there while the tree scrolls underneath, five at a time with a Show more for the rest. They are not in the search drop-down as well: one list in two places is one too many, and the drop-down copy was the one that disappeared the moment you typed. See The note header for the key and its ordering.

Down the right rail:

Section What it is
On this page The note's outline, from its headings.
About this note Word count, reading time, section count, and the date.
Linked from Every published note that links here.
Related What the note is near: notes that share links with it rather than linking to it directly.
Local graph The neighbourhood, laid out at build time and emitted as an SVG. No graph library ships to the reader.

A Read / Markdown switch. Markdown mode shows the note's own text, minus what the rendering hides. It is not a developer's curiosity: the whole promise of the project is that the vault is the source and the site is a rendering of it, and a reader who wants to quote a passage or lift a table is better served by text than by a selection dragged out of styled HTML.

A note has source behind it, so the switch is there. The browse page, the tag pages and the generated homepage do not, so it is not.

A theme toggle. Light and dark are both built, and a reader's choice is remembered from page to page. Which one a first-time visitor lands in is Settings → Appearance → Opens in, their own system setting by default. See Themes and your own CSS.

Links that go nowhere

Wikilinks become relative URLs. A link to a note that is not being published degrades safely rather than producing a dead link. It stays readable as text. This is what makes a partial publish usable: you can publish a third of a vault whose notes all link to each other.

Attachments

The files a page points at are carried and their references rewritten: images, and for an as-is page the scripts and stylesheets too. See Publishing a built app.
